AI

By “AI”, we mean by all AI algorithms. And in the context of this blog, we not only mean by “AI algorithm” as a way to simulate intelligence (talked about it in one of previous blog titled “AI - Mathematical Formulation of Intelligence”), but actually we are using the phrase to represent any of the discoveries (research papers) in AI.

Let’s first talk about AI research. Technology research or AI research is about finding “logical” solutions to problems. Logical solutions are proposed by researchers in research papers and expands research community’s knowledge logically. “Logical” here is nothing but whatever they are proposing has to be newer i.e, different than proposals in existing papers and is either better or has the potential to be better than other existing solutions. Whenever some researcher’s paper is reviewed for publication in a conference or journal, it is checked that whatever the paper is proposing is logical or not.

There is always an algorithm along with solution in an AI research paper. Either solution is the algorithm itself or there is algorithm to support the solution. In any case, we are calling the algorithm an “AI algorithm” just because it is there in AI research paper.

If solution is AI algorithm itself then that algorithm is either intelligence-simulating algorithm (ex: new model, new loss, new optimiser etc.) or that algorithm is an improvement over existing AI algorithms (recursive definition). Improvement could either be in explanation, evaluation, efficiency or performance. Some examples of algorithms for explanation are: explaining a layer (like batch norm) or activation functions (like why relu over sigmoid) or optimisers (like why SGD generalise better than Adam in some cases) or why neural network can learn and generalise etc.

If solution is not algorithm itself but some theoretical findings, then in this case, algorithm is there to support the theoretical findings, i.e., algorithm is used to do experimentation to check whether theoretical findings are true or not by finding evidence. If true then to what extent it is true. We are focusing on algorithm because “mastering” it meaning including whole research paper and solution. This will get more clear in next section.

Depending on how new is the solution, it can be classified as iterative or novel research.

  • “novel” - significant novel change in an existing algo to improve it or completely new algorithm.

Now, let’s talk in detail about “AI algorithm”. Any AI algorithm is nothing but an advanced machine learning (ML) algorithm. ML is a way via computers can learn. “ML algorithm” is nothing but implementation of ML concepts. And any ML algorithm is nothing but an application or combination of high-dimensional (or “HD” for this blog) maths tools. High-dimensional is anything which is more than 1 dimensional.

Note:- Mathematics required to master ML and AI is Linear Algebra, Probability, Statistics, Multivariate Calculus, Optimisation etc. I have used the term “maths fundamentals” in my previous blogs to refer to this required maths. Here I will be using another term for “maths fundamentals”, which is “high-dimensional maths”. Both terms represent the same maths and can be used interchangeably. Not all but most of maths fundamentals is high-dimensional. “High-dimensional” term is just used to emphasis the main difficulty in mastering maths fundamentals.

Basic ML algorithms are simple algorithms like linear regression, logistic regression, shallow neural networks etc. They do not simulate human-like intelligence but they have very useful many real-world applications like house price predictions, forecasting etc. If one is not interested in their application then still one should understand these algorithms because they can help to easily understand ML concepts.

Advanced ML Algorithms or “AI algorithms” are ML algorithms, so same ML concepts are used. Advanced ML algorithms are similar to basic ML algorithms. The only difference is that there is a lot more use of HD maths in AI algorithms. Because AI algorithm implement human-like learning and simulate human intelligence which is more complex than simple problems solved by basic ML. Complexity which AI algorithm deals with is high dimensional highly nonlinear functions whose either input or output or both lies in HD spaces. These HD spaces can be of images, text, speech, music, videos, etc. To deal with such complex functions, one need to use a lot more HD maths. More use of HD maths tools will be like more application of matrix multiplication (E.g., in deep neural networks), use of function approximation which is taylor approximation (E.g. for approximating loss surface for analysis or for second-order optimisation which involves hessian) or use of multivariate normal distribution for approximating high-dimensional probability distribution etc.

There are only few parts of high dimensional maths for which we can draw analogies with 1 dimensional or 2 or 3 dimensional maths and hence they are intuitve. Rest (majority) of parts of high dimensional maths are highly unintuitive, which makes it difficult to grasp, which requires significant efforts to understand.

How research is done?

Note:- This section may get heavily edited in later versions.

In this section, we will talk about how research is done to better understand what we mean by mastering a topic.

To solve a problem and find a logical solution, a researcher has to first choose some tools. Depending on how much new or different the solution a researcher want, we will chose the tools for solving. Choosing the tools means assuming that these are the only [best?] tools available to solve the problem. And we have to find the better solution given these tools.

More complex the tools we choose, more closer we are to logically solve the problem. More complex the tools, more heavylifting is outsourced to the tools and we have to only do small tweaks. More complex the tools we choose, more potential other complex tools we are discarding. More complex the tools we choose, more cons it will have along with more pros because finding the ideal tool out of exponentially large number of all possible complex tools is very difficult. Hence, more complex the tools, more our assumption is strong and less of it is true. Hence, more complex the tools, lesser the solution is logical - less new as well as less better. Less new because with only small tweak we are able to get to the solution. Less better because of only small tweak, we are only iteratively improving the solution, hence only able to suppress the cons little bit and not entirely.

More simpler choosen tools are, less weaker the assumption is. Meaning, less number of total tools exist, so more chances that we are picking the ideal tool or whatever tools which are picked they are closer to being ideal. Which means, we are more away from solving the problem, bigger solution space, more control we have in our hand to find more logical solution, more control we have in our hand to fix cons of existing complex tools. Because we know the existing complex tools available and their cons, so keeping this in mind, we can first find different and better complex tools for our problem using simpler tools. And then using better complex tools to solve the main-problem in more logical way.

At the simplest level, very few tools exist and all are ideal, perfect tools with no scope of improvement, meaning these are the tools which is going to solve any problem perfectly and lead to most logical solution ever exist. If tools are simplest then we are solving the problem from first principle because then we are not assuming, we are sure that this is the only way to solve the problem logically and therefore solving in most logical way possible.

More simpler the tools, we are more closer to solving the problem from first principle. But at the same time, it becomes difficult to find the logical solution. But even closer to solving from first principle is enough, i.e., using simpler tools and not simplest, is enough. Below even if a problem is solved from simpler tools and not simplest, we will call it “solving from first principle”. In other way, by solving from first principle, we mean that we are not using the most complex tools.

[continuum between applied to fundamental] In research, every paper do choose some tools and then by using it, researchers propose something new on top of it. Depending on the level of complexity of the choosen tools, the “assumption” as explained above will be weaker or stronger and hence the corresponding paper could be part of “fundamental” research or be part of “applied” research.

Applied research is application specific where researchers want to solve a given real-world problem in best way possible from whatever complex tools available. So, they will choose most complex tools which are available like transformer, a complex neural network and assume that they are good enough in themselves and uses it to solve some real-world problem like disease diagonosis.

On the other hand, researchers in fundamental research do not focus on solving any problem from given most complex tools. Rather, they try to come up with better complex tools so that any real-world problem can be solved in a more logical way (more newer and more better). That is, they try to solve a problem from first principle by choosing simpler tools. E.g. they will not assume that current transformers are good enough, they will rather just assume that neural network, a less complex tool or a simpler tool, are good and try propose something which improves upon current transformers. A even more fundamental research wont asssume that neural network are good and they will propose some alternative of neural networks. Some other fundamental research will assume neural network and but propose a totally different way of training them. More simpler the tools, more fundamental the research is.

  • Different level of complexity of tools keeps the research going by allowing iterative development. Without different level of tools complexity, only very few number of good fundamental papers will come out. Here, goodness is in terms of whether it can be used in applied research or not.
